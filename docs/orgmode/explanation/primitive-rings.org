#+TITLE: Primitive rings and the full graph
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

A ring is primitive when the graph distance between every pair of its
vertices equals the shorter distance along the ring. That is Franzblau's
shortest-path test. A chordless ring can fail it: the missing shortcut
need not be an edge of the ring.

* Distances, then the source

The search builds a ring from one source, the lowest-indexed member of
that ring. Two facts have to hold at once.

Distances are taken on the whole graph. A shortcut may pass through a
vertex whose index is below the source. Hiding that vertex makes the
opposite vertex look farther away than it is, and a ring that is not
primitive is kept.

After those distances are known, every vertex below the source loses
its level. No path is extended through that vertex, so it is not
written onto a ring this source emits. The ring is still reported once,
from its lowest member.

* Worked example

Number the vertices of a hexagon ~1-2-3-4-5-6~, and join a new vertex
~0~ to the opposite vertices ~1~ and ~4~.

A walk that refuses to enter vertex ~0~ reports distance 3 between 1
and 4. The hexagon then looks primitive, because each pair appears to
sit at its ring distance.

The full graph has the path ~1-0-4~, so the distance is 2. The ring
distance is 3, and the hexagon is not primitive. The two pentagons
~0-1-2-3-4~ and ~0-1-6-5-4~ are primitive. A chordless search keeps the
hexagon as well, because no edge joins two non-consecutive vertices of
the hexagon. The primitive search, and OVITO FindRings on this bond
list, both reject the hexagon and keep the pentagons.

~tests/test_ring_hop.cpp~ is that fixture. The same rule is what the
depth-10 census matches against OVITO at ring sizes 4 through 10.
